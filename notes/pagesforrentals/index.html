The quiet calendar behind PagesforRentals

June 2026

PagesforRentals started with a quiet calendar. Last summer, our apartment in Malta began well: we had bookings, good feedback, and the early signs that the place could work as a short-term rental. Then the calendar went quiet, and from October to February we had no bookings at all. That was enough to make me look more closely at which parts of the business were actually ours to change.

Seasonality was not going away, Airbnb's algorithm was beyond me, and I obviously could not make someone choose Mellieħa over another town. What I could change was how the apartment showed up online. We have a property manager because we are not in Malta day to day, and someone local needs to handle guests, cleaning, check-ins, and all the practical details that make a stay work. That arrangement makes sense, but it also means we cannot easily change how the apartment presents itself on Airbnb or Booking.

The parts we could own

In hindsight, that limited how quickly we could react, but it also pushed me towards building the parts we could own. A direct website felt like the obvious place to start. I never expected it to replace Airbnb or Booking; the point was to give the apartment a home of its own, with clearer photos, better information, a bit more personality, and somewhere it could show up outside the usual platform search results.

Once that existed, it was natural to think about what happened after someone booked as well. The website did not have to stop being useful at the point where a reservation was made.

A page for the stay itself

Because we are not there in person, and because guests usually communicate through the property manager, I wanted a simple way for them to find practical information without digging through a printed folder or waiting for a reply. A link in the welcome email made more sense than a binder on a table, so I built a guest page with the things people are likely to need during a stay: WiFi, check-in notes, house rules, local information, emergency details, and a few recommendations nearby. Nothing elaborate, just one useful place for the small questions that inevitably come up.

A way back for returning guests

The direct site matters after checkout too. I kept thinking about repeat guests: on Airbnb we are one listing among hundreds, while on our own site the apartment has a name, a place to return to, and a direct enquiry path for someone who already knows they liked it. That can work better for us and for the guest. If someone comes back directly, we save platform fees and can share some of that saving with them, which makes the site a small part of the business rather than just a brochure.

A second rental made the pattern obvious

Around the same time, Košice Central was coming together: a small apartment in the old town of Košice, in a very different market but with much the same basic need. It needed a clean, trustworthy home online that was not simply another platform listing, and it needed the same sort of guest page because the practical problem was familiar. Guests still need clear information, and hosts still end up answering the same questions over and over again.

Thinking about both properties made the pattern much easier to see. Independent rentals do not only need a nice landing page; they benefit from a small web presence around the whole stay, from the point someone first discovers the property through to the practical details after booking and, ideally, a way to find it again later.

Naming the place properly

Košice Central also made me think differently about naming. The name is simple, but it feels like a place: specific, memorable, and easy to point someone towards. Next to it, Malta Luxury Apartment started to feel generic. It described the property well enough, but it did not give it much identity.

That eventually led to Town's End Malta. I wanted both rentals to have a stronger presence of their own rather than existing mainly as pages on Airbnb or Booking, and Košice Central had already shown me how much more coherent that feels when the name, website, and guest information all belong to the same place.

What PagesforRentals is for

That is where PagesforRentals came from. It was not a big product idea or something I arrived at through a pitch deck; I was trying to solve our own problem carefully, and eventually realised that the same shape kept appearing in both rentals.

The idea is fairly simple. An independent rental should have somewhere of its own to be found and understood, somewhere useful for guests once they have booked, and a place they can remember if they want to come back. That is what I'm trying to build with PagesforRentals: simple, useful websites for independent rentals that deserve a little more than a listing on someone else's platform.

PagesforRentals is live now, as are the two rental sites that shaped it: Town's End Malta and Košice Central. Together they are a pretty good example of the idea: a direct home for the rental, useful information for guests, and a clearer way for people to come back.
